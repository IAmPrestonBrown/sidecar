# Example study: how small bakeries decide what to bake

This is a fictional study that ships with Sidecar as an example. The bakeries and
people in it are made up.

## Purpose

Learn how owners of small, independent bakeries decide how much of each item to bake
for the next day, and what happens to what does not sell.

Every outcome is valid: support, contradiction, a narrow segment, a different
problem, or no problem worth solving.

## H1 - Plans come from memory

Most owners set tomorrow's quantities from what they remember selling recently, not
from written sales records.

## H2 - Leftovers are a steady loss

Unsold items at closing are a noticeable cost in most weeks, not just on unusual days.

## H3 - Big misses have outside causes

Weather, school holidays, and local events explain most days that end with far too
much or too little, and owners can usually name the cause afterward.

## H4 - A short daily suggestion would be used (solution)

Owners would check a suggested bake list each evening if it took under a minute to
read. Evidence for H1 to H3 is not evidence for H4.

## Competing explanation

"Leftovers are a choice. Full shelves sell more, and day-old discounts or donations
recover most of the cost."

## Evidence rules

- What someone did last week counts for more than what they usually do.
- One bakery is not a pattern; two people at one bakery are not independent.
- "We throw a lot away" is not a number. Ask for the last specific day.

## Interview approach

Start with: "Could you walk me through how you decided what to bake for today?"
Do not bring up apps, forecasting, or software before they do.

## Segments

- Bakery cafe
- Retail-only bakery
- Retail and wholesale
- Market stall or pop-up
